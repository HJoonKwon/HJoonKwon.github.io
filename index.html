Joon Kwon

M.S. Robotics, Carnegie Mellon University · AirLab.

Robots today can recognize almost anything they see, yet they rarely understand how the world will physically respond when they act in it. I want to close that gap, so that robots can take on physical work in the real world, not only in controlled demonstrations. For my Ph.D., I want to build multimodal representations that drive a robot's actions end to end. They should remain inspectable, yielding explicit, measurable estimates of not only the world's geometry but also its physical behavior, such as how heavy an object is or whether the ground will hold. They should also carry their own uncertainty: as the robot acts, the surprise between what it predicted and what it measures updates the representation and corrects the action. Disaster response is the hardest test of this idea: a robot must act correctly the first time, without the chance to probe first.

I am an M.S. Robotics student in CMU's AirLab, advised by Prof. Sebastian Scherer and Dr. Wenshan Wang. Before CMU I was at Boeing AI, supervised by Jay Oh, using CV/ML to improve airplane manufacturing quality at the factory, and at Nearthlab, under CTO Youngsuk Chung, carrying drone-perception PoCs into shipped products.

My recent work, TartanMatch, extends dense correspondence beyond RGB to thermal, event camera, and LiDAR modalities, so a robot can still see and localize when an RGB camera cannot. That need came directly from the DARPA Triage Challenge, where I led the development of the monocular 3D mapping and target localization system that reports each casualty's GPS position to first responders from both EO and IR imagery, with under 50 cm error.

02Selected publications

TartanMatch: Towards Universal Dense Correspondence Across Modalities

Submitted to IEEE RA-L
H. Kwon*, J. Cai*, R. Li*, K. Bhandari, G. Hemkumar, P. Maheshwari, Y. Qiu, Y. Zhang, S. Scherer, W. Wang. Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 2026. *Equal contribution.

Robots combine several sensors to work across conditions, but most dense-correspondence methods handle only RGB-to-RGB or a few modality pairs, and training one model per pair scales poorly and forgoes transfer between pairs. TartanMatch is one model for all 25 ordered pairs among RGB, event, thermal, depth and projected LiDAR: an RGB-pretrained correspondence model adapted to every pair inside a single shared network with lightweight modality adaptation and a mix of real and synthetic data.

  • Zero-shot on real-world benchmarks, it cuts endpoint error against the strongest baselines by 61.9% for cross-modal and 49.6% for same-modal matching.
  • Joint training across all pairs beats pair-specific training, most of all on the hard cross-modal pairs: one model, broad coverage, state-of-the-art accuracy.

Autonomous Guidance for Multi-revolution Low-thrust Orbit Transfer via Reinforcement Learning

H. Kwon, S. Oghim, H. Bang. 31st AAS/AIAA Space Flight Mechanics Meeting, 2021. AAS 21-315 · Advances in the Astronautical Sciences, vol. 176, pp. 783–798.

A Soft Actor-Critic agent chooses thrust direction across an entire multi-revolution transfer at constant thrust magnitude, meeting the terminal boundary condition while minimising flight time, with a gradient-form reward that makes the long-horizon problem tractable.

03Projects

2025 – present · DARPA Triage

Target Re-ID & Localization for Search and Rescue

An end-to-end system that detects, localizes and re-identifies casualties for first responders.

  • Real-time 3D mapping and localization: monocular SLAM fused with GPS through pose-graph optimization.
  • Every casualty reported live in GPS coordinates (latitude, longitude, height), with the camera frame it was detected in.
  • Each reported location must fall within 2 m of the casualty's surveyed GPS position.
  • Duplicate reports of the same casualty are penalized heavily, so re-identification has to hold for static and moving targets alike.
2021 – 2024 · Nearthlab

Autonomous Wind-Turbine Inspection

One product, owned across the whole pipeline, so that robots do the dangerous work instead of people.

  • Autonomous drone flight collecting blade imagery in physically harsh environments.
  • Label definition, annotation and data curation; defect detection and classification.
  • Coverage check: every part of the blade must be photographed, verified before the drone leaves the turbine.
  • I developed the image-stitching pipeline for the textureless blades, combining GPS, camera geometry and learning-based feature matching, so every defect is localized in 3D on one stitched blade.
  • Inspection 7× faster than rope access, because the drone covers a blade in minutes and the stitched image is reviewed once instead of frame by frame. The matching work behind it placed 7th of 642 teams in the CVPR 2022 Image Matching Challenge and won a gold medal (10th) in the 2023 edition.
