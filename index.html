Joon Kwon

M.S. Robotics, Carnegie Mellon University · AirLab.

I work on giving machines a sense of space that survives the sensor. My research extends dense correspondence and geometric foundation models past RGB — into event, thermal and LiDAR — so perception degrades gracefully where robots are actually needed: at night, in smoke, in the field.

Before CMU I was at Boeing AI, supervised by Jay Oh, using CV/ML to improve airplane manufacturing quality at the factory; at Nearthlab, under CTO Youngsuk Chung, carrying drone-perception PoCs into shipped products; and at KAIST, advised by Prof. Hyochoong Bang, on reinforcement learning for spacecraft guidance.

For a Ph.D. I want to push toward spatial representations learned once and held across modality, time and viewpoint — and to keep validating them on real hardware, the way I have for the last five years.

Autonomous Guidance for Multi-revolution Low-thrust Orbit Transfer via Reinforcement Learning

H. Kwon, S. Oghim, H. Bang — 31st AAS/AIAA Space Flight Mechanics Meeting, 2021. AAS 21-315 · Advances in the Astronautical Sciences, vol. 176, pp. 783–798.

A Soft Actor-Critic agent chooses thrust direction across an entire multi-revolution transfer at constant thrust magnitude, meeting the terminal boundary condition while minimising flight time — with a gradient-form reward that makes the long-horizon problem trainable.
